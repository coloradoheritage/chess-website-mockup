Before You Choose

Your Homeschool, Your Curriculum — Why Your Accountability Partner Matters

Colorado families have real choices when it comes to homeschool accountability. District-based programs can appear attractive — but understanding the full picture is essential before you decide.

Home Resources Why Independent School?

A Decision Worth Understanding

The Appeal — and the Reality

When a family first explores homeschooling in Colorado, the path can seem straightforward: simply notify your local public school district and let them serve as your accountability partner. Some districts actively promote this arrangement, offering access to resources, elective classes, extracurricular activities, and even curriculum materials — often at no direct cost to the family. It can seem like the most practical, lowest-friction choice.

And in some ways, it is. We want to be honest about that.

But for Christian families committed to raising their children in the nurture and admonition of the Lord — who chose homeschooling precisely because they wanted to direct their child's education according to their convictions — this arrangement deserves a much closer look. The accountability partner you choose is not a neutral decision. It determines who has authority over your curriculum, who receives government funding for your child's education, and whose values ultimately define the educational relationship your family is in.

What Most Families Don't Know

School Districts Are Funded Per Student

Colorado's public school funding formula is based on Average Daily Membership (ADM) — in simple terms, each enrolled student generates a funding allocation for that district. When a homeschooling family notifies their district and enrolls in a district-based accountability program, the district counts that child in their enrollment figures.

This means the district receives state funding for your child's education — even though you are the one doing the teaching, purchasing the curriculum, and providing the instruction at home.

The resources and services districts offer to homeschoolers through these programs are typically funded, at least in part, through the per-pupil dollars your child generates for them. The arrangement is not as cost-free to your family as it may appear — the cost is paid in the form of enrollment data, curriculum oversight, and a formal relationship with the district.

Per-Pupil State Funding

Every student enrolled in a Colorado public school district — including those in district-affiliated homeschool programs — generates per-pupil funding for that district from the state.

Enrollment Incentive

Districts have a direct financial incentive to grow their homeschool program participation. More families enrolled means more state dollars — which is why many districts actively promote their homeschool programs.

With Funding Comes a Relationship

An accountability relationship funded through public enrollment is not the same as a private arrangement between your family and a like-minded institution. The terms are not yours to set.

The Practical Implications

What You May Be Agreeing To

District-based accountability programs vary across Colorado's 178 school districts. Some are minimally involved; others are quite hands-on. But when you enter a district accountability arrangement, you are voluntarily entering into an ongoing relationship governed by public education standards, policies, and — in some cases — values that may differ significantly from your own.

Curriculum Approval Requirements

Many districts require that curriculum materials used by enrolled homeschool families be reviewed and approved. Materials that are explicitly Christian in content — or that present history, science, or human sexuality from a biblical worldview — may be restricted or disallowed under district standards.

Standardized Testing & Reporting

Families in district programs typically must participate in district-administered or district-selected standardized testing — with results reported to and retained by the district as part of their enrollment data. This places your child's academic performance under district oversight.

Teacher Evaluations

District accountability programs often require annual evaluations of your child conducted by district-employed or district-approved teachers. These educators bring a professional framework shaped by public school training, standards, and in many cases, state-mandated ideological commitments.

District Policies Apply to You

When you enroll with a public school district, you become subject to the policies of that district — including non-discrimination policies, inclusion mandates, and required training or acknowledgment of district values statements that may directly conflict with biblical convictions on gender, sexuality, and family.

Rules Change When Leadership Changes

District programs are governed by elected boards and appointed administrators. The policies that apply to your family today may change dramatically when school board membership shifts — and they often do. A program that appears family-friendly under one administration may be quite different under the next.

The Door Opens Both Ways

Once you establish an enrollment relationship with your district, the district has a legitimate basis to inquire into your educational program, request records, and potentially flag your family for review if their standards are not met. You have invited them in.

A Biblical Perspective

The Question Behind the Question

Many Christian families choose to homeschool because they understand that education is never religiously neutral. Every curriculum, every question, every framework for understanding history, science, and humanity either assumes the existence and authority of God or does not. Deuteronomy 6 calls parents to teach their children diligently — not on Sundays only, but when they sit, when they walk, when they rise, and when they lie down. That is a total vision of education.

The question then is not merely logistical — it is theological. When you choose an accountability partner, you are choosing who has authority to define, evaluate, and oversee the education of your child. For a Christian family, this is a question of stewardship.

Public school districts are funded by the state and governed by the state's values. They are legally prohibited from treating Christianity as the authoritative framework for truth. Their standards for curriculum, evaluation, and conduct are written from a secular premise — and increasingly, from a secular premise that is actively hostile to orthodox Christian convictions about human identity, the family, and morality.

"Train up a child in the way he should go; even when he is old he will not depart from it." — Proverbs 22:6

We believe that training begins with choosing who is in the room — including who is watching over that training from the outside.

The Alternative

What Colorado Heritage Offers Instead

Colorado Heritage has served Colorado homeschooling families as a private independent school since 1991. We exist to be the accountability partner that strengthens your family's educational mission rather than constraining it.

Full Legal Compliance

CHESS fulfills Colorado's compulsory attendance requirement under C.R.S. § 22-33-104.5 as a recognized private independent school. Your family is fully covered — legally and completely.

Your Curriculum, Always

We do not review, approve, or restrict the curriculum you use at home. Whether you use a classical Christian program, Charlotte Mason, traditional textbooks, or an eclectic mix — the choice is entirely yours.

K–12 Academy Enrichment

Independent School families may also enroll their students in our K–12 Academy classes — taught from a distinctly Christian worldview by committed Christian educators. It's the best of both.

Official Diploma Option

The CHESS High School Diploma Program provides an official private school diploma and transcript for students who complete our diploma requirements — a credential that carries real weight with colleges and employers.

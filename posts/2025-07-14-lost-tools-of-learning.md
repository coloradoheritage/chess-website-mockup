Dorothy Sayers asked the question in 1947. It remains just as urgent today.

## The Trivium and What We've Forgotten

Classical education rests on a framework called the Trivium: Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric. These are not simply subjects — they are stages of learning that correspond to the natural development of the child's mind.

- **Grammar** (roughly ages 9–11): The child absorbs facts, language, and foundational knowledge with remarkable ease.
- **Logic** (roughly ages 12–14): The child begins to question, argue, and reason. This is the age of "why."
- **Rhetoric** (roughly ages 15–18): The young adult learns to express truth persuasively, with grace and conviction.

Modern education largely abandoned this framework in the 20th century. The result, Sayers argued, is students who have been taught *what* to think but not *how* to think.

## The Christian Distinctive

Classical education without Christianity produces eloquent pagans. The Trivium equips the mind — but the mind must be oriented toward something. At Colorado Heritage, we believe that something is the knowledge of God.

Every subject, rightly taught, points to Him. Mathematics reveals the order He built into creation. Literature explores the human condition He designed. History traces His providential hand through time. Science uncovers the mechanisms of a world He spoke into existence.

This is not a constraint on education. It is its completion.

## What This Means for Homeschooling Families

You do not need a classical school to give your children a classical education. Many of our Independent School families use classical curricula at home — and our Academy classes are designed to complement that approach.

The tools of learning have not been lost. They have simply been set aside. We invite you to pick them up again.

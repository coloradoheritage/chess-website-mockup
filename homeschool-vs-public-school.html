Academic Achievement

Decades of peer-reviewed research consistently show that homeschooled students perform significantly above average on standardized tests compared to their public school peers. Studies by Dr. Brian Ray at the National Home Education Research Institute (NHERI) show homeschoolers typically score in the 65th to 80th percentile on standardized academic achievement tests, compared to the 50th percentile for public school students.

Importantly, this advantage holds regardless of family income, whether or not the parent is a certified teacher, and the amount of regulation in the state. The single most consistent predictor of homeschool student success is parental involvement — the very thing homeschooling maximizes.

Homeschooled students also tend to complete assignments with more depth and retain information longer, because instruction is personalized and can be paced to mastery rather than to a one-size-fits-all schedule.

Key Research Findings

  • Homeschoolers score 15–30 percentile points above public school peers on standardized tests (Ray, 2010)
  • This gap persists regardless of gender, ethnicity, or socioeconomic status
  • Teacher certification of the parent has no statistically significant effect on outcomes
  • More regulated states do not produce better homeschool outcomes than less regulated ones
  • Homeschoolers read more books and engage more deeply with primary sources (NHERI, 2017)

The Socialization Question

"But what about socialization?" — It's the most common objection to homeschooling, and the research doesn't support it.

The Myth

The concern is that homeschooled children will be isolated, lacking the social skills needed to function in adult society. This assumption rests on the idea that being confined to a classroom with 25–30 same-age peers for 7 hours a day is the normal, healthy model of social development.

But that model — peer-centric, age-segregated, and institution-dependent — is historically and globally unusual. Most of human history saw children socialized in multi-age family and community settings.

The Reality

Research consistently finds that homeschooled students have higher self-esteem, greater civic engagement, and better social maturity than their public school peers (Medlin, 2013). They participate in more community service, vote at higher rates, and report greater life satisfaction.

Through co-ops, sports leagues, church activities, and programs like CHESS Academy classes, choir, and drama, homeschool students interact regularly with peers and adults in meaningful, multi-generational settings.

College Admissions & Career Outcomes

Homeschool graduates are not just admitted to colleges — they tend to thrive once they get there. Studies show homeschool graduates earn higher college GPAs, graduate at higher rates, and report greater satisfaction with their education than traditionally schooled students.

The Distinctly Christian Case

Beyond the academic and social data, there is a deeper reason Christian families choose to homeschool: the formation of a child's soul.

Integrated Biblical Worldview

In homeschooling, history is not just facts — it is Providence. Science is not just data — it is the order of Creation. Mathematics is not just calculation — it is the logic of God's mind. Every subject can be taught in the light of Scripture.

Protection of Impressionable Years

The years between 5 and 18 are the most formative of a human life. Public school culture — peer pressure, secular curriculum, moral relativism — exerts enormous pressure on children during these years. Homeschooling keeps that formation in the hands of those God charged with it: parents.

Deuteronomy 6 in Practice

"You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise." Homeschooling is this command made practical and daily.

Faith Retention

Research consistently shows that children raised in intentionally Christian environments with active parental discipleship are significantly more likely to retain and practice their faith as adults. Christian homeschooling is one of the most effective tools for this.

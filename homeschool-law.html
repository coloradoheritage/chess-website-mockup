The Short Version

Colorado Protects Your Right to Homeschool

Colorado has long been recognized as one of the most homeschool-friendly states in the country, consistently classified among states with low homeschool regulation. The state legislature has made it clear that parents have the fundamental right to direct the education of their children — and the law reflects that conviction.

Colorado's compulsory school attendance law requires that children between the ages of 6 (by August 1) and 17 receive an education. However, the law does not require that education to take place in a public school. Families have three recognized legal pathways to fulfill this requirement — and the choice of which pathway to use is yours.

What Colorado does not require is equally important: the state does not mandate a specific curriculum, does not require parents to hold a teaching certificate, and does not prescribe how or where instruction must take place. These freedoms are yours to steward as the parent.

This page is for informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Laws can change, and every family's situation is different. For authoritative legal guidance, we recommend contacting HSLDA (Home School Legal Defense Association). CHESS Independent School members receive a 15% discount on HSLDA membership.

Your Three Legal Options

Colorado law provides three distinct pathways for homeschooling families to fulfill the compulsory attendance requirement. Each carries different responsibilities, freedoms, and trade-offs.

1

Report to Your Public School District

File a notification with your local public school district stating your intent to homeschool. The district serves as your accountability partner, and you may be subject to curriculum approvals, standardized testing through the district, and district-employed teacher evaluations.

Key Considerations

  • →District receives per-pupil state funding for your child
  • →Curriculum may require district approval
  • →Annual standardized testing may be required
  • →Evaluation by a licensed teacher
Recommended
2

Enroll in a Private Independent School

Enroll your family in a private independent school like Colorado Heritage. The school serves as your accountability partner and school of record — fulfilling all state requirements while leaving curriculum selection, daily scheduling, and instructional approach entirely in your hands.

Key Benefits

  • ✓Full curriculum freedom — you choose
  • ✓No government or district oversight
  • ✓Official school records & transcripts
  • ✓Faith-integrated accountability partner
  • ✓HSLDA membership discount (15% off)
3

Hire a Colorado-Certified Teacher

Engage a Colorado state-certified teacher to evaluate and oversee your child's education. This option is less commonly used but available to families who prefer a direct professional relationship rather than institutional enrollment.

Key Considerations

  • →Teacher must hold current CO state certification
  • →Less common; fewer support resources
  • →No official school records or transcripts
  • →Annual teacher evaluation required

The Law in Detail

What the Law Requires

172 Days / 688 Hours of Instruction

Your homeschool program must provide a minimum of 172 school days or 688 instructional hours per year. This mirrors the public school calendar and ensures students receive sufficient instructional time.

Required Subject Areas

Colorado law specifies that instruction must include: communication skills (reading, writing, speaking), mathematics, history, civics, literature, science, and regular physical activity. The law does not dictate which curriculum, textbook, or method you use to cover these areas.

Annual Evaluation

Each year, students must be evaluated through either a standardized assessment or a portfolio review conducted by a qualified evaluator. At Colorado Heritage, we help coordinate this requirement for our enrolled families.

Record Keeping

Families are required to maintain attendance records, immunization records, and test scores. As your school of record, Colorado Heritage maintains these files on your behalf — keeping your family compliant and organized.

What the Law Does NOT Require

  • A specific curriculum, publisher, or textbook

  • A teaching certificate or credential for the parent

  • A fixed daily schedule or school hours

  • A dedicated classroom or physical school location

  • District-approved instructional materials

  • Standardized test scores reported to any government agency

Compulsory School Age in Colorado

Under Age 6

No compulsory attendance requirement. Colorado does not require children under 6 to be in any school program. Early education is entirely at the family's discretion.

Ages 6–17

Colorado's compulsory attendance period. Children who turn 6 by August 1 must be enrolled in an educational program for that school year. This is when CHESS Independent School enrollment applies.

Age 17+

Once a student turns 17, Colorado's compulsory attendance requirement ends. Students pursuing the CHESS Diploma Program continue enrollment voluntarily through graduation.

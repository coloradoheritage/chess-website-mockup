Should your child go to college at all — and if so, how do you choose one that will strengthen their faith rather than tear it down? A guide for families thinking through the years after high school.

First Things First

Should Your Child Go to College at All?

It is worth saying plainly: college is not the only faithful path, and it is not the measure of a young person's worth or godliness. For generations, families have assumed that a four-year degree is the automatic next step. We would gently encourage you to slow down and ask a better question — what has God called this particular child to do?

A degree is genuinely valuable for many vocations — medicine, law, engineering, teaching, ministry, and more. But God also calls young men and women to skilled trades, apprenticeships, the military, entrepreneurship and family business, missions, and the God-honoring work of building a home and raising the next generation. Each of these can be pursued with excellence, to the glory of God.

Questions worth praying through as a family:

  • What gifts, interests, and callings has God given this child?
  • Does the path they are considering actually require a degree — or is there a wiser route?
  • Is your child spiritually grounded enough to stand firm away from home?
  • What debt would be involved, and is it wise stewardship (Proverbs 22:7)?
  • Have you sought the counsel of your pastor and other godly mentors?

If, after prayer and counsel, college is the right path for your student — the next question is just as important as the first: which one?

Choosing Wisely

How to Choose a College Faithfully

The college years shape a young person's beliefs, friendships, and direction for life. Choose an institution that will build on the foundation you have laid — not one working to tear it down. Here is what we believe matters most.

Doctrinal Faithfulness

Read the school's statement of faith carefully. Does it affirm the inerrancy of Scripture, the virgin birth, the deity and bodily resurrection of Christ, and salvation by grace through faith alone? A vague or shifting statement is a warning sign.

A Faithful Local Church — A Must

This is non-negotiable. A campus ministry is not a substitute for the local church. Before enrolling, confirm there is a sound, Bible-believing church nearby where your student can be planted, sit under faithful preaching, and be known and shepherded.

Spiritual Climate

A statement of faith on paper is not the same as a living, godly culture. Ask about chapel, the spiritual life of the faculty, and how the school disciples students. Visit if you can. Talk to current students and their parents.

Academic Fit & Calling

Does the school offer strong programs in the field God seems to be leading your student toward? Faithfulness and academic rigor are not in tension — pursue both.

Wise Stewardship

"The borrower is slave to the lender" (Proverbs 22:7). Weigh the cost honestly. Crushing debt can shape a young person's choices for a decade. Consider scholarships, working through school, and starting at a lower-cost path where wise.

A Place to Stand Firm

Choose a setting that reinforces your family's convictions rather than constantly assaulting them. The goal is a launching pad for a life of faithful service — not four years of spiritual survival.

Where We Stand

Why We Recommend Only Bible-Believing Christian Colleges

Everything Colorado Heritage does is built on the conviction that all of life — including education — belongs to Jesus Christ. We have spent years helping you raise your children in the nurture and admonition of the Lord. We will not, at the very end, point them toward institutions designed to undo that work.

For that reason, we recommend only colleges that publicly affirm the fundamentals of the historic Christian faith — the inerrancy of Scripture, the virgin birth of Christ, His full deity and bodily resurrection, and salvation by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone.

And we say this plainly and without apology: we cannot in good conscience recommend secular state universities. We do not say it out of fear, but out of love and conviction. These institutions are built on a worldview that frequently stands in direct opposition to the faith — and to send a young believer into that environment as the assumed default is not a step we will encourage.

"See to it that no one takes you captive by philosophy and empty deceit, according to human tradition... and not according to Christ."

— Colossians 2:8 (ESV)

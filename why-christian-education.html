Shaping Your Child's Worldview for Christ

Who Is Shaping Your Child's Worldview?

Every child is being formed by something. The question Christian parents must answer is whether that formation is happening deliberately, toward Christ — or by default, toward something else.

Section 01

Who Is Shaping Your Child's Worldview?

Christian parents today have more educational choices than ever before. Whether considering public school, private school, homeschooling, or a hybrid homeschool program, parents must ask an important question: which educational environment will best help their child develop a biblical worldview and grow in wisdom, character, and faith?

Many parents evaluate schools based on academics, extracurricular opportunities, and college preparation. While those things certainly matter, there is an even more fundamental question that should be considered:

Who is shaping your child's worldview?

Every parent wants their child to succeed. Yet success is never merely a matter of knowledge. It is also a matter of character, values, beliefs, and purpose. The reality is that every educational system teaches far more than reading, writing, and arithmetic. Every school is shaping how students understand themselves, the world around them, and their place within it.

At CHESS, we believe Christian education should do more than prepare students for tests, careers, or college. Christian education should help students understand God's world, know His truth, and learn how to glorify Him in every area of life.

As Christians, we believe that parents are the primary educators of their children. Scripture commands parents to diligently teach God's truth to the next generation (Deuteronomy 6:6–9). Fathers are instructed to bring up their children "in the discipline and instruction of the Lord" (Ephesians 6:4, ESV). While parents may enlist the help of teachers, pastors, and mentors, God has entrusted the responsibility for a child's education and discipleship primarily to the family.

This does not mean that parents must do everything themselves. It does mean, however, that parents remain accountable before God for what their children are taught and how they are formed.

"Train up a child in the way he should go; even when he is old he will not depart from it."

— Proverbs 22:6

Questions Every Education Answers

  • What is truth?
  • What does it mean to be human?
  • What is the purpose of life?
  • Where does morality come from?
  • What should students ultimately live for?

Every school answers these — whether it acknowledges it or not.

Section 02

Education Is Never Neutral

Schools teach much more than academics.

Students learn values, authority, morality, identity, citizenship, and what is considered worthy of praise or criticism. These lessons are often taught through what educators call the "hidden curriculum"—the countless assumptions and messages communicated through classroom culture, curriculum choices, and institutional priorities.

Consider a simple example. A school teaches punctuality by requiring students to arrive on time. It teaches responsibility by expecting assignments to be completed. It teaches authority by determining whose voices should be trusted and whose ideas should be followed.

Every school teaches these things because education is inherently formative.

Historically, many educational reformers understood this reality. Advocates of the common school movement believed that education could shape society and produce better citizens. They viewed schools as a means of promoting a particular vision of morality and social order.

One of the most influential educational leaders in American history, William Torrey Harris, believed that education should cultivate moral discipline and train young people to become productive citizens. His educational philosophy emphasized order, structure, punctuality, and the formation of character through the school system.

Whether one agrees with his conclusions or not, Harris understood something that many modern parents overlook: education always shapes the soul as well as the mind.

Section 03

Christian Education vs. Public Education

The difference between Christian education and public education is not simply the presence or absence of Bible classes. The deeper difference lies in the worldview from which every subject is taught.

Every educational system answers important questions:

  • What is truth?
  • What does it mean to be human?
  • What is the purpose of life?
  • Where does morality come from?
  • What should students ultimately live for?

Christian education begins with the conviction that God is the Creator of all things and that His Word is the ultimate standard of truth. A biblical worldview recognizes that every subject—from mathematics and science to history and literature—exists under the Lordship of Jesus Christ.

This does not mean that Christian schools have a monopoly on truth. Christians affirm that all truth is God's truth. However, Christian education seeks to understand that truth within the framework God Himself has revealed in Scripture.

For Christian parents, the question is not simply where their children will learn facts. The question is whether those facts will be taught within a worldview that honors Christ and reinforces the values being taught at home.

The Question Is Not Merely What We Learn, But Why

Every educational system teaches a purpose for life.

Students naturally want to know why what they are learning matters. Education is not simply the accumulation of information. It is the process of understanding who we are, why we exist, and what we ought to do with the lives God has given us.

The Christian answer is fundamentally different from the answer offered by the world.

We believe that mankind was created in the image of God and therefore possesses inherent dignity and worth. Our value does not come from our accomplishments, talents, wealth, or social status. It comes from being created by God Himself.

Yet we also recognize that sin has corrupted mankind. Because of the Fall, human beings do not naturally drift toward truth and righteousness. We need God's grace, God's truth, and ultimately God's redemption through Jesus Christ.

Christian education therefore seeks not merely to prepare students for a career, but to help them understand their purpose before God and their place in His world.

Section 04

Every Subject Belongs to Christ

Christian education is not merely Bible class added to an otherwise secular curriculum.

Christ is Lord over every subject.

Mathematics reveals the order of God's creation. Science studies the world He made. History displays His providence among the nations. Literature explores the joys, struggles, and hopes of people made in His image. Music allows us to express wonder and praise to our Creator.

God is the God of mathematics as surely as He is the God of theology. He is the Creator of every truth that can be known.

Because of this, every subject ultimately points back to Him. The purpose of education is not merely to master facts, but to cultivate wonder at God's creation and gratitude toward the God who made it.

Education Is Training in Wisdom

Our culture has more information than any civilization in history. Yet information alone does not produce wisdom.

Students must learn how to distinguish truth from falsehood, wisdom from folly, and righteousness from sin.

Scripture teaches that true wisdom begins with the fear of the Lord (Proverbs 9:10). Christian education seeks to train students not only to know facts, but to think biblically about every area of life—to recognize error, discern truth, and stand firmly upon God's Word.

"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom."

— Proverbs 9:10

Section 05

A Partnership With Parents

At CHESS, we believe that parents—not the state, not the school, and not educational experts—have the primary responsibility for raising and educating their children.

That is why we operate in loco parentis—in the place of the parent.

Parents voluntarily delegate a portion of their child's instruction to teachers who share their convictions and support their goals. The teacher's role is not to replace parents, but to come alongside them in the task God has given them.

We seek to reinforce the biblical worldview already being taught in the home. We strive to provide academic excellence while helping students grow in wisdom, character, and love for God.

In this way, the school exists for the benefit of parents, helping them fulfill their God-given responsibility of training their children in the fear and admonition of the Lord.

Education Is More Than Social Progress

Many educational reformers believed that education could solve society's problems and create a better world. While education is a tremendous blessing, Christians recognize its limitations.

Education can teach skills. It can cultivate discipline. It can transmit knowledge. But it cannot change the human heart.

Only the gospel of Jesus Christ can do that.

Without Christ, education may simply produce more sophisticated sinners. True transformation comes only through the saving work of God. This is why Christian education must be rooted in the truth of God's Word and the hope of the gospel.

Preparing Students for Their Calling

Education should prepare students not merely for college, but for vocation.

God has given every individual unique gifts, abilities, and opportunities for service. Whether a student becomes a pastor, farmer, engineer, teacher, homemaker, business owner, craftsman, or missionary, he is called to glorify God through faithful stewardship of the gifts entrusted to him.

Christian education helps students develop their God-given abilities so that they may serve Christ faithfully in every area of life.

Section 06

Education as Discipleship

Ultimately, education is a form of discipleship.

Every teacher is shaping what students believe about truth, goodness, beauty, and purpose. The question is not whether children will be discipled. The question is who will disciple them and toward what end.

The Westminster Shorter Catechism begins with a simple but profound question:

What is the chief end of man?
Man's chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy Him forever.

At CHESS, we believe that this is the ultimate purpose of education as well.

Our goal is to teach young people that Christ is Lord over every area of life, so that they might be renewed after His image, walk in wisdom, discern truth from error, and glorify God through the gifts and opportunities He has given them. We seek to come alongside parents in this task, helping students grow in knowledge, character, and faith as they learn to enjoy God through the world He has made.

How CHESS Helps

Supporting Christian Homeschool Families in Northern Colorado

Many Christian parents desire to be actively involved in their children's education but recognize that they cannot teach every subject themselves. Homeschooling can be both rewarding and challenging, especially as students progress into upper-level courses. That is why CHESS exists.

As a Christian homeschool program serving families throughout Fort Collins and Northern Colorado, CHESS operates alongside parents rather than replacing them. We provide academic instruction from a biblical worldview while allowing parents to remain the primary educators of their children.

Our goal is to support homeschool families by providing excellent teachers, strong academics, meaningful friendships, and a Christ-centered learning environment that reinforces what students are already learning at home and in their local church.

We believe the best educational partnerships occur when parents, teachers, and churches work together to help students grow in wisdom, knowledge, and godliness.

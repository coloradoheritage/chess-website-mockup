The Foundation of Everything

Why Christian Education Is So Important

Education is never neutral. Every approach to learning carries a worldview. Christian education simply makes its worldview explicit — and anchors it in the truth of Scripture.

Section 01

Education Is Never Neutral

One of the most dangerous ideas in modern education is the claim that schooling can be "neutral" — that facts can simply be transmitted without a framework, that knowledge exists independent of meaning, that a child can be educated without being formed.

This is not true. It has never been true.

Every teacher brings assumptions to the classroom. Every textbook reflects a philosophy. Every curriculum makes choices about what is worth knowing, what is worth valuing, and what kind of person the student should become. The question is never whether education has a worldview — only whose worldview it carries.

Christian education does not claim to have no worldview. It claims to have the right one. It begins with the conviction that God is the Creator and Sustainer of all things, that His Word is truth, and that the purpose of education — like the purpose of all human life — is to know Him and to glorify Him.

"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom; all those who practice it have a good understanding." — Psalm 111:10

This is not a limitation on education. It is its proper starting point.

"All truth is God's truth."

Augustine observed that wherever truth is found — in mathematics, in science, in history, in literature — it belongs to God. Christian education is simply the commitment to teach all subjects in the light of that truth.

The Two Questions Every Education Answers

  • 1
    What is real? — Is the universe the product of chance, or of a Creator who gives it meaning?
  • 2
    What is a human being? — Are we evolved animals, or image-bearers of God with dignity, purpose, and responsibility?

Every school answers these questions — whether it acknowledges it or not.

Deuteronomy 6:6–7

"These commandments that I give you today are to be on your hearts. Impress them on your children. Talk about them when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up."

Section 02

Who Is Responsible for Education?

Scripture is clear: the education of children is the responsibility of parents. Not the state. Not institutions. Not culture. Parents.

Deuteronomy 6 does not instruct the Israelites to build a school system and hand their children over to it. It instructs parents to talk about God's commands constantly — in the home, on the road, in the morning and evening. Education, in the biblical vision, is not a task parents delegate. It is a calling they inhabit.

This does not mean parents must do everything alone. It means they must remain in authority over their children's formation — choosing instructors who share their convictions, curriculum that reflects their values, and a community that reinforces rather than undermines what is taught at home.

The modern public school system, whatever its merits in other respects, was not designed with this vision in mind. It was designed for mass instruction, driven by state standards, and shaped by cultural assumptions that have drifted far from a biblical foundation.

Homeschooling — and the network of private school support that makes it legally secure and academically excellent — is the practical expression of this biblical calling. It is parents saying: we take seriously the charge God has given us.

Section 03

What Does "Christian Education" Actually Mean?

Christian education is not simply adding a Bible class to a secular curriculum. It is approaching every subject through the lens of a biblical worldview — so that all learning points toward its proper end.

History as Providence

History is not a sequence of random events driven by economics and power. It is the unfolding of God's providential plan. Christian education teaches children to see His hand in the rise and fall of nations, the lives of individuals, and the arc of redemption.

Science as Discovery

Science is the exploration of God's creation. Every natural law is a reflection of the order He built into the universe. Christian education teaches children that science and faith are not enemies — that discovering how things work is a form of worshiping the One who made them work that way.

Literature as Humanity

Great literature explores the human condition — sin, redemption, love, loss, courage, and cowardice. Christian education teaches children to read through a biblical lens: to recognize truth in unexpected places, to name evil when they see it, and to long for the restoration Scripture promises.

Mathematics as Order

That abstract mathematical structures describe physical reality is, as physicist Eugene Wigner wrote, "unreasonably effective." For the Christian, this is not a mystery — it is evidence that the same Mind that designed mathematics designed the universe it describes.

Arts as Worship

Human beings are made in the image of a creating God. Art, music, drama, and beauty are not luxuries added onto serious education — they are expressions of what it means to be human. Christian education treats beauty as a moral and theological category, not merely an aesthetic one.

Civics as Stewardship

Christians are called to be good citizens and faithful stewards of the cultural institutions God has placed in their care. Christian education teaches students to engage the world — not to retreat from it — with wisdom, conviction, and grace.

Section 04

The Stakes Are High

A child receives roughly 15,000 hours of formal schooling between kindergarten and high school graduation. These are the most formative years of their life — the years in which their fundamental beliefs, habits of mind, and moral intuitions are shaped.

Research consistently shows that the values instilled during childhood and adolescence are remarkably persistent into adulthood. Faith that is genuinely integrated into education — not compartmentalized to Sunday mornings — produces adults who hold and live that faith far more consistently.

Conversely, children educated in a framework that treats faith as irrelevant to real knowledge, who are surrounded by peers for whom faith is similarly marginal, frequently graduate not just uneducated in Christianity but actively formed against it.

This is not alarmism. It is a realistic assessment of what education does. Education forms people. The question is always: into whose image?

Section 05

Education as Formation, Not Just Information

The ancient Greeks distinguished between paideia — the comprehensive formation of a human being in virtue, wisdom, and culture — and mere technical instruction. Christian education stands squarely in the tradition of paideia. It is not simply concerned with what students know. It is concerned with who they become.

A student can memorize the periodic table, complete calculus problems, and write grammatically correct essays — and still be thoroughly unprepared for the most important questions of their life: Who am I? Why am I here? What is worth living for? What is wrong with the world, and what can be done about it?

"The goal of education is not to fill a bucket but to light a fire." — attributed to W.B. Yeats

Christian education aims to light a fire in the right direction — toward God, toward wisdom, toward love of neighbor, toward the kind of life that brings flourishing both to the individual and to the world they inhabit.

This means that character formation is not a supplement to academics — it is academics. Honesty in reporting results, diligence in difficult work, humility before a subject larger than yourself, gratitude for the gift of a mind — these are not "soft" additions to a rigorous education. They are its essential shape.

Why This Matters for Homeschooling Families

Homeschooling parents understand this instinctively. You are not trying to replicate a classroom in your living room. You are trying to raise a whole person — a son or daughter who loves God, loves truth, loves people, and is equipped to serve the world in whatever calling God gives them.

That is a bigger vision than any institutional school system is designed to fulfill. It requires the integration of faith, learning, character, and community that is uniquely possible when parents remain the primary architects of their children's education.

This is why Colorado Heritage exists. Not to replace parents, but to support them — with legal standing, academic structure, a community of like-minded families, and 35 years of experience navigating the journey from kindergarten through graduation.

Shaping Your Child's Worldview for Christ

Who Is Shaping Your Child's Worldview?

Every child is being formed by something. The question Christian parents must answer is whether that formation is happening deliberately, toward Christ — or by default, toward something else.

Section 01

Who Is Shaping Your Child's Worldview?

Every child is being shaped by something. From the moment they wake up to the moment they fall asleep, they are receiving messages about what is real, what is valuable, who they are, and what the world is for. The question is not whether your child will develop a worldview. They will. The question is: who will shape it?

In an age of social media, secular curriculum, and a culture that has largely abandoned the fear of God, the formation of your child's mind and heart is not something that happens by accident. It happens by intention — either yours, or someone else's.

Christian education is the deliberate choice to bring your child's entire intellectual life under the lordship of Christ. It is the conviction that education is not merely the transfer of information, but the formation of a person — and that formation matters eternally.

"See to it that no one takes you captive through hollow and deceptive philosophy, which depends on human tradition and the elemental spiritual forces of this world rather than on Christ." — Colossians 2:8

The stakes could not be higher. And the responsibility could not be more clearly placed: on parents who love their children and who answer to God for how they raised them.

"Train up a child in the way he should go; even when he is old he will not depart from it."

— Proverbs 22:6

Three Competing Voices

  • 1
    Scripture — God's Word as the foundation for all of life and learning
  • 2
    Culture — Media, peers, and institutions with their own competing assumptions
  • 3
    Parents — The God-appointed stewards of their children's formation

Christian education puts the right voice in the center.

Christian vs. Public Education

  • God as Creator is the starting point — not a footnote
  • Morality grounded in Scripture, not shifting cultural consensus
  • The student is an image-bearer of God with eternal dignity and purpose
  • Parents, not the state, bear ultimate responsibility for education

Section 02

Education Is Never Neutral

One of the most persistent myths in modern education is the claim that schooling can be "neutral" — that knowledge can be transmitted without a framework, that facts exist independent of meaning, that a child can be educated without being formed in a particular direction.

This is not true. It has never been true.

Every teacher brings assumptions to the classroom. Every textbook reflects a philosophy. Every curriculum makes choices about what is worth knowing, what is worth valuing, and what kind of person the student should become. The question is never whether education has a worldview — only whose worldview it carries.

Public education, by design, excludes God from the conversation. This is not neutrality — it is a choice. It teaches students to think about history, science, literature, and ethics as if God were irrelevant. Over thousands of hours, that assumption becomes formative.

Christian education does not claim to have no worldview. It claims to have the right one: that God is Creator and Sustainer of all things, that His Word is truth, and that the purpose of all learning is ultimately to know Him and to glorify Him in every area of life.

"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom; all those who practice it have a good understanding." — Psalm 111:10

Section 03

Every Subject Belongs to Christ

The question is not merely what we learn, but why. Christian education integrates faith into every discipline — not as an add-on, but as the lens through which all knowledge is understood and all truth is received.

History as Providence

History is not a sequence of random events. It is the unfolding of God's providential plan. Christian education teaches students to see His hand in the rise and fall of nations and the arc of redemption through time.

Science as Discovery

Science is the exploration of God's creation. Every natural law reflects the order He built into the universe. Faith and science are not enemies — understanding how things work is a form of worshiping the One who made them that way.

Literature as Humanity

Great literature explores the human condition — sin, redemption, love, courage, and loss. Christian education teaches students to read through a biblical lens: to recognize truth, name evil, and long for the restoration Scripture promises.

Mathematics as Order

That abstract mathematical structures describe physical reality is, as physicist Eugene Wigner wrote, "unreasonably effective." For the Christian, this is not a mystery — it is evidence that the same Mind designed both mathematics and the universe it describes.

Arts as Worship

Human beings are made in the image of a creating God. Art, music, and beauty are not luxuries — they are expressions of what it means to be human. Christian education treats beauty as a moral and theological category.

Civics as Stewardship

Christians are called to be good citizens and faithful stewards of the institutions God has placed in their care. Christian education prepares students to engage the world — not retreat from it — with wisdom, conviction, and grace.

Section 04

Education Is Training in Wisdom

Christian education is more than academic excellence. It is the formation of wisdom — the ability to see the world rightly, to think clearly about what matters, and to act in a way that honors God and serves others.

A student can master calculus, ace every standardized test, and earn acceptance to elite universities — and still be thoroughly unprepared for the most important questions of their life: Who am I? Why am I here? What is worth living for? What does God require of me?

Education that does not address these questions is not truly education at all. It is job training. Christian education is concerned not only with what students know, but with who they become — with the formation of character, conscience, and conviction that will carry them through every season of life.

This is education as discipleship. And it is the only kind of education that is adequate to the calling God has placed on every human life.

The most formative years of a human life. What they add up to depends on the worldview behind them.

"Education is more than social progress. It is preparation for eternity."

Faith retention is significantly higher among students whose faith was woven into their education — not compartmentalized to Sunday mornings and treated as separate from "real" learning.

Section 05

A Partnership With Parents

Scripture is clear: the education of children is the responsibility of parents. Not the state. Not institutions. Not culture. Parents.

Deuteronomy 6 does not instruct parents to build a school system and hand their children to it. It instructs them to talk about God's commands constantly — in the home, on the road, in the morning and evening. Education, in the biblical vision, is not a task parents delegate. It is a calling they inhabit.

"These commandments that I give you today are to be on your hearts. Impress them on your children." — Deuteronomy 6:6–7

This does not mean parents must do everything alone. It means they must remain in authority over their children's formation — choosing instructors who share their convictions, curriculum that reflects their values, and a community that reinforces rather than undermines what is taught at home.

Christian education at its best is a partnership: parents who take seriously the charge God has given them, supported by teachers and a school community who share their vision and walk alongside them.

Preparing Students for Their Calling

Christian education does not exist to produce scholars in isolation. It exists to prepare students for whatever calling God places on their lives — as parents, professionals, artists, tradespeople, ministers, or leaders — equipped with a biblical worldview, a trained mind, and a formed conscience.

The goal is not to produce students who can pass tests. The goal is to produce students who can think — who can ask the right questions, weigh evidence against a standard of truth, engage the world with confidence, and remain anchored to Christ when that world pushes back.

That kind of preparation does not happen by accident. It is the fruit of years of deliberate, Christ-centered formation — the kind that Colorado Heritage has been committed to since 1991.

A Practical Guide

How to Start Homeschooling

Thinking about homeschooling your children? This guide walks you through every step — from understanding the law to choosing curriculum, staying organized, and getting the community support you need.

Home Resources How to Start Homeschooling

You Can Do This

The Most Important Decision You'll Make for Your Child

Homeschooling is one of the most rewarding things a family can do — and one of the most misunderstood. The decision is not just academic. It is a statement about what you believe education is for, who is responsible for it, and what you want your child's formation to look like.

Whether you're just starting to consider it or you're ready to pull your child out of school tomorrow, this guide will help you navigate every practical step with confidence.

6 Steps to Getting Started

1

Understand Your State's Homeschool Law

Every state has different homeschool laws. In Colorado, children ages 6–17 must attend school, and homeschooling is a fully legal option. You can homeschool by enrolling in a private school (like CHESS), by declaring as a homeschool, or by other means. Knowing your state's options is the critical first step.

Colorado Homeschool Law Details HSLDA State-by-State Guide
2

Choose How You Will Comply Legally

In Colorado, the simplest and most legally secure path is to enroll your child in a private school like Colorado Heritage. As your school of record, we handle attendance and record-keeping on your behalf. You don't notify the public school district — you report to us instead. This is called an "umbrella school" or "independent school" model, and it's what our Independent School program provides.

Why Choose an Independent School?
3

Choose Your Educational Philosophy

There are several main approaches to homeschooling. Most Christian families gravitate toward one or a combination of these:

  • Classical Education — Emphasizes the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), great books, and rigorous thinking. Excellent for Christian formation.
  • Traditional / Textbook — Follows a structured curriculum with textbooks and grade-level standards, similar to a conventional school setting.
  • Charlotte Mason — Living books, nature study, narration, and short lessons. Emphasizes beauty and wonder in learning.
  • Unit Studies / Eclectic — Combines multiple approaches around topics or themes. Very flexible for multi-age families.
Our Christian Curriculum Guide
4

Select Your Curriculum

This is one of the most exciting (and occasionally overwhelming) parts of starting out. There are hundreds of excellent Christian curriculum options. You don't need to buy everything at once — start with the core subjects (math, reading/language arts, Bible) and add from there. See our Curriculum Guide for recommendations by subject and grade level.

Pro tip: many families also supplement home curriculum with one or two CHESS Academy classes per week, especially for subjects that benefit from a professional instructor (science labs, foreign language, writing workshops, etc.).

5

Set Up a Record-Keeping System

Colorado law requires homeschooling families to keep attendance records and provide them to their school of record. When you enroll with CHESS, we guide you through what records to keep and collect them from you annually. Good records become essential if your student plans to apply to college, pursue dual enrollment, or enter the military.

Basic records to maintain: daily attendance log, curriculum list by subject, samples of student work (optional but recommended), immunization records, and standardized test results (required starting in 3rd grade).

6

Find Your Community

Homeschooling does not mean educating in isolation. The best homeschool families are deeply connected to a community of like-minded believers. CHESS provides that community through our K–12 Academy classes, choir, drama productions, graduation ceremony, and the broader network of families we serve.

Other great community resources: your local church, HSLDA, Christian homeschool co-ops in your area, and online communities for Christian homeschool families.

Common Concerns — Answered

"I'm not a trained teacher."

You don't need to be. The most important qualifications for homeschooling are love for your child and commitment to their education. Curriculum is designed to guide you. For subjects where you want extra help, CHESS Academy classes are there to support you.

"What about socialization?"

Homeschooled students regularly outperform peers in social development. Through Academy classes, sports, church, co-ops, and community activities, homeschool students interact with a wide range of people — just not confined to one age group in one institution. See the research →

"Will colleges accept my student?"

Yes. Homeschool graduates are accepted at colleges across the country every year, including selective universities. A CHESS private school transcript and diploma, combined with strong standardized test scores, gives your student a strong admissions profile.

"How much time will it take?"

Far less than you think. Elementary homeschooling often takes 2–4 hours of focused instruction per day. High school students become increasingly self-directed. Many homeschool families find they have more family time, not less, than they did in the conventional school system.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the most common questions about enrolling with Colorado Heritage, our programs, costs, and homeschooling in general.

Enrollment

How do I enroll at Colorado Heritage?

Enrollment is done through our online application. Visit our Apply page to complete the enrollment form. After submission, our office will review your application and contact you within 3–5 business days to finalize your enrollment and collect the required paperwork (immunization records, attendance records, etc.).

When can I enroll?

Independent School enrollment is open throughout the year, though we encourage families to enroll before the school year begins. Academy class registration opens each spring for the following year — watch our announcement bar at the top of the site for registration dates. Enrollment and re-enrollment must be renewed every year.

What paperwork do I need to enroll?

For the Independent School, families need to submit: immunization records for each student, an attendance log for the school year, and any standardized test scores (once testing age is reached). Our office will walk you through exactly what is needed after you apply. See our Acceptance Policy for full eligibility requirements.

Do I have to re-enroll every year?

Yes. Enrollment in the CHESS Independent School is renewed on an annual basis. Families must re-enroll each year and submit updated paperwork by the required deadline. Failure to re-enroll may result in your family not being considered officially enrolled for that school year, which could create truancy issues. We send reminders to all current families.

Costs & Fees

How much does it cost to enroll in the Independent School?

The annual Independent School enrollment fee is $125.00 — that covers your entire family for the school year. This is a non-refundable fee due at enrollment. A late re-enrollment fee of $50 applies after September 30. See our full fee schedule for all current rates.

Are there scholarships or financial assistance available?

Colorado Heritage seeks to make Christian homeschool support accessible to all families. Please contact our office to discuss financial hardship situations. We also offer an HSLDA membership discount of 15% to all enrolled families.

How much do Academy classes cost?

Academy class tuition ranges from $270–$480 per class per semester, depending on grade level and days per week. A $75 technology fee applies per student enrolled in Academy. See the complete fee schedule for a full breakdown by course type.

What is the Diploma Program fee?

The Diploma Program has a $50 one-time application fee, plus $170 per semester ($340/year). The graduation ceremony is an additional $150 for Diploma Program members. Official transcripts are available separately. See the Diploma Resources page or our fee schedule for full details.

Programs

What is the difference between the Independent School and the Academy?

The Independent School is our "umbrella school" service — you educate your children at home, and CHESS serves as the school of record, handling attendance reporting, record-keeping, and legal compliance. The Academy is a set of actual classes held in-person (in Greeley, CO) that your child can attend once or twice a week in subjects of your choosing. Many families use both together. See our Programs page for full details.

Do I have to enroll in the Independent School to take Academy classes?

Yes. Students enrolling in Academy classes must also be enrolled in the CHESS Independent School. This ensures proper record-keeping, legal compliance, and that your student is part of our school community. The combined cost is still significantly less than any private school alternative.

What grades and subjects does the Academy offer?

The Academy serves grades K–12 with a rotating schedule of subjects. High school offerings include core academics (English, math, science, history), Bible and worldview, foreign languages, fine arts, and electives. See the current course descriptions and class schedule for what is available this year.

Do I have to live near Greeley to participate?

For Academy classes, yes — they are held in-person in Greeley, Colorado, so students need to be local or able to commute. However, the Independent School and Diploma Program are available to families anywhere in all 50 states. Many out-of-state families enroll with us for record-keeping and diploma services.

Diplomas & Transcripts

Will colleges accept a CHESS diploma and transcript?

Yes. CHESS issues official private school transcripts that are recognized by colleges and universities for admissions purposes. We have been issuing private school credentials since 1991 and our graduates have been accepted to universities across the country. We recommend students also take the SAT, ACT, or CLT to further strengthen their applications.

What are the different diploma levels?

CHESS offers four diploma levels: Diploma, Honors Diploma, High Honors Diploma, and Highest Honors Diploma. Each level has increasing academic requirements. See the Diploma Resources page for a full breakdown of requirements for each level.

Can I transfer credits from another school into the Diploma Program?

Yes, transfer credits from accredited academic institutions and prior homeschool records can be incorporated into your CHESS transcript. Transcript transfer fees apply (see the fee schedule). Contact our office to discuss your specific situation.

Can my student attend the graduation ceremony?

The graduation ceremony is open to Diploma Program graduates, Independent School families, and even non-enrolled families on a case-by-case basis. It's a beautiful, Christ-centered ceremony held annually in the Greeley area. See the Graduation Resources page for full details.

Legal & Compliance

Does enrolling in CHESS fulfill Colorado's compulsory attendance law?

Yes. Enrollment in the Colorado Heritage Independent School satisfies Colorado's Compulsory School Attendance Law for children ages 6 through 17. Instead of reporting to a public school district, your family reports to CHESS as your school of record. See our Colorado Homeschool Law page for full details on your legal rights and responsibilities.

At what age does my child need to be enrolled?

Colorado law requires school attendance for children who are 6 years old by August 1st of the school year. Many families choose to enroll earlier to begin building good academic habits, but legal compulsory attendance begins at age 6.

Do I need to do standardized testing?

Colorado law requires homeschool students to undergo standardized testing or evaluation beginning in 3rd grade and every other year thereafter (grades 3, 5, 7, 9, 11). Colorado Heritage offers testing services to our enrolled families. See our Testing Services section for available tests and fees.

Out-of-State Families

Can I enroll if I don't live in Colorado?

Yes! Our Independent School and Diploma Program are open to homeschooling families in all 50 states. You continue homeschooling in compliance with your own state's laws, while CHESS serves as your private school of record for enrollment documentation, transcripts, and diplomas. In-person Academy classes are only available to families in the Greeley, CO area.

Does enrolling with CHESS satisfy my state's homeschool law?

Homeschool law varies by state. Enrolling in a private school umbrella program like CHESS satisfies the legal requirements in many states (including Colorado), but you should verify your own state's specific requirements. We recommend consulting HSLDA.org for state-by-state guidance. CHESS enrolled families receive a 15% HSLDA membership discount.

Can an out-of-state student attend the graduation ceremony?

Absolutely. Out-of-state families enrolled in the Diploma Program are warmly encouraged to attend. Many families travel to Greeley specifically for the graduation ceremony. It is one of the most meaningful days of a homeschool family's journey. See the Graduation Resources page for details.

Christian Education

What is Christian education?

Christian education is an approach to learning that places God's Word at the center of all subjects and uses a biblical worldview as the lens for understanding everything — history, science, literature, mathematics, and the arts. It is not simply a Bible class added to a secular curriculum. It is the conviction that all truth belongs to God, and that education should form the whole person — mind, character, and faith — to know Him and glorify Him in every area of life. See our full article: Who Is Shaping Your Child's Worldview?

Why is a biblical worldview important for education?

Every education carries a worldview — a set of assumptions about what is real, what is true, and what matters. Education is never neutral. A biblical worldview begins with God as Creator and Sustainer of all things, grounds morality in Scripture rather than shifting cultural consensus, and understands each student as an image-bearer of God with eternal dignity and purpose. Without this foundation, students may master facts and skills while remaining completely unprepared for the questions that matter most: Who am I? Why am I here? What does God require of me?

Can Christian education prepare students for college?

Absolutely. Christian homeschool graduates consistently perform at or above their peers on standardized tests and in college. CHESS students who complete the Diploma Program graduate with an official transcript recognized by colleges nationwide. More importantly, a Christ-centered education produces students who can think clearly, write persuasively, reason carefully, and engage ideas with confidence and conviction — exactly the skills that lead to success in higher education and beyond.

How does CHESS support Christian homeschooling families?

Colorado Heritage provides three layers of support for Christian homeschooling families: (1) Legal standing — as your private school of record, we cover your family's compliance with Colorado homeschool law (and provide transcript/diploma services for families in all 50 states); (2) Academic enrichment — our K–12 Academy offers Christ-centered classes taught by dedicated instructors every Tuesday and Thursday; and (3) Community — choir, drama, graduation, and a network of families who share your convictions. See all programs →

What makes Christian education different from public education?

The difference is foundational, not cosmetic. Public education, by legal design, excludes God from the conversation — treating faith as a private belief separate from "real" knowledge. Christian education begins with God as the source of all truth and integrates that conviction into every subject. Where public education may teach history as the product of impersonal forces, Christian education teaches it as the unfolding of God's providence. Where public education treats ethics as cultural consensus, Christian education grounds it in Scripture. The result is not just a different set of content, but a fundamentally different kind of student — one formed to see the world rightly and live accordingly.
